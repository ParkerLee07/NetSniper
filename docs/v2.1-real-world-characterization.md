# NetSniper v2.1 Real-World Characterization

Status: manual engineering observations; not a benchmark or corpus input.

## Scope

Three authorized single-device scans were reviewed against the frozen v2.1
classifier candidate. The captures represented a router, a client endpoint, and
a printer. Raw bundles, network identifiers, hostnames, paths, run IDs, and
service fingerprints are not committed.

The scans used the accurate profile in an unprivileged context. Discovery, TCP
service collection, and passive-neighbor collection completed. Requested OS
and UDP-lite collectors produced no results, so platform and negative-evidence
conclusions remain limited.

## Router observation

The router exposed a web management endpoint and gateway-oriented context, but
the collected evidence did not independently establish a canonical device
family with sufficient confidence. The classifier remained conservative rather
than asserting a platform or deployment class.

This observation identifies a sensor-evidence limitation. It does not justify
forcing a router classification from operator knowledge alone.

## Client endpoint observation

The endpoint was reachable but exposed none of the monitored TCP services. The
classifier returned unknown family and platform with no service roles.

That result is defensible under the current evidence contract. A reachable
endpoint with no observable classification evidence cannot be assigned an OS or
device family solely to avoid an unknown result.

## Printer observation

The printer exposed standard web and printing services, including IPP and raw
printing. The classifier identified the printer family and print-service role at
review confidence. Generic HTTPS-based rules also produced weak unrelated
secondary role candidates.

The primary direction is useful, while the unrelated secondary candidates are a
specific behavior to review before release. Any runtime correction must be
separately proposed, approved, and protected by a deterministic regression
case.

## Interpretation boundary

These three observations are not statistically representative and do not
support an accuracy percentage. They are used only to prioritize focused
engineering review while preserving conservative uncertainty behavior.
