# NetSniper v2.1 Accuracy and Calibration Gates

Status: Stage 0D baseline.

## 1. Dataset splits

Every corpus fixture is assigned exactly one split:

- `development`: visible during rule design and classifier calibration.
- `evaluation`: not used to tune scoring. It is executed during release-candidate
  evaluation.
- `regression`: permanently protects a known behavior, compatibility contract,
  or previously identified defect.

The initial 30-case corpus is assigned as follows:

- 14 development fixtures
- 12 evaluation fixtures
- 4 regression fixtures

All three sanitized real-world placeholders are in the evaluation split.

The assignments freeze at the first commit that implements the v2.1
classifier. Moving a fixture after that point requires a documented reason.
Changing evaluation ground truth requires resetting the evaluation baseline and
rerunning the release-candidate assessment.

The evaluation split is a process holdout, not a secrecy guarantee. Repository
visibility does not permit a truly blind dataset. Contributors must not tune
weights or rules in response to evaluation results.

## 2. Release-blocking corpus gates

The following gates apply to active fixtures and are exact, not statistical:

- Deterministic replay rate: 100%
- Host-retention rate: 100%
- Schema-validity rate: 100%
- Required uncertainty-reason compliance: 100%
- Confidence-cap compliance: 100%
- Strong-contradiction review behavior: 100%
- Legacy-projection match rate: 100%
- Fixture expectation pass rate: 100%
- False high-confidence classifications: 0
- Unknown/review controls incorrectly emitted as `classified`: 0

A planned fixture does not count toward these gates. A fixture becomes active
only after source evidence, normalized evidence, ground-truth provenance, and a
recorded timestamp are present.

A planned evaluation fixture may have sealed source and normalized inputs during
preparation. It remains excluded from replay and release metrics until an
explicit reviewed activation checkpoint. Preparation must not store classifier
results.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Host-retention rate

Discovered inventory hosts present in the finalized bundle divided by discovered
inventory hosts. The only acceptable release value is 1.0.

### Fixture expectation pass rate

Fixtures whose family, roles, platform, decision, confidence range,
uncertainty reasons, contradiction handling, and legacy projection all satisfy
their manifest expectations, divided by active fixtures.

### False high-confidence classification

A family, role, or platform emitted with confidence 90–100 and decision
`classified` when it conflicts with ground truth or is explicitly forbidden by
the fixture.

### False classified unknown

A fixture whose expected family decision is `unknown` or `review`, but which is
emitted as a classified non-unknown family.

### Classified precision

Correct `classified` predictions divided by all predictions emitted as
`classified`. Precision is measured separately for family and role axes.

### Family macro recall

The arithmetic mean of per-family recall for families that satisfy the minimum
sample floor. `unknown` is evaluated as an outcome but is not used to inflate
supported-family recall.

### Expected calibration error

Predictions are grouped into confidence bins. The weighted absolute difference
between average confidence and observed correctness is the expected calibration
error.

## 4. Statistical claim floor

The starter corpus is a release-regression baseline. It is too small to support
broad claims such as “98% accurate.”

Published statistical accuracy claims require at least:

- 100 active evaluation fixtures
- 10 evaluation examples for every family named in the claim
- 50 high-confidence predictions for a high-confidence precision claim
- 100 predictions for a calibration claim

Until those floors are met, release documentation may state only that NetSniper
passes its declared corpus, regression, compatibility, and safety gates.

## 5. Statistical quality targets

Once the claim floor is met:

- Classified family precision: at least 95%
- Classified role precision: at least 95%
- High-confidence precision: at least 98%
- Family macro recall: at least 80%
- Expected calibration error: at most 0.10

These targets do not override exact safety gates. A model with acceptable
aggregate precision still fails release if it loses a host, violates a
confidence cap, mishandles a strong contradiction, or produces any false
high-confidence fixture result.

## 6. v2.1 release readiness

Before NetSniper v2.1.0 can be released:

1. All 30 starter fixtures must be either active or explicitly retired with a
   written reason.
2. At least 12 evaluation fixtures must be active.
3. All 3 sanitized real-world evaluation fixtures must be active.
4. Every release-blocking corpus gate must pass.
5. Evaluation results must not have been used to retune the candidate.
6. Failures discovered during evaluation must be converted into regression
   fixtures before the candidate is rebuilt.
7. The rebuilt candidate must be evaluated from a reset baseline.

The statistical claim floor is not required for v2.1.0, but the release must not
make unsupported percentage-accuracy claims.
